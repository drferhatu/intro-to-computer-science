<Step n={5} title="Part A · Warm-up: meow, with a custom block" time="10 min" id="part-a">

Rebuild the last version of the lecture's meow program from memory, in a **new** Scratch project (sign in at [scratch.mit.edu](https://scratch.mit.edu) → **Create**):

1. *My Blocks → Make a Block*, name it `meow` and give it a number input called `n` (tick **Add an input · number or text**).
2. Define it: `repeat n { play sound Meow until done; wait 1 seconds }`.
3. Main script: `when green flag clicked` → `meow 3`.

Click the flag: three meows, one second apart. Change 3 to 5 without touching the definition. That is abstraction working for you.

Then **Share → Copy Link**, open `README.md` in your codespace, and replace the placeholder after `Meow:` with your link:

```text
Meow: https://scratch.mit.edu/projects/123456789/
```

Stuck? Load the lecture's [meow4.sb3](/scratch/meow4.sb3) into Scratch (**File → Load from your computer**) and compare.

</Step>

<Step n={6} title="Part B · Your own project" time="35 min" id="part-b">

Build a small program of your own that has **all** of these:

- at least one **custom block** (with or without an input);
- at least one **variable** that changes while the program runs (a score, a counter, a countdown);
- at least one **loop** (`forever`, `repeat`, or `repeat until`);
- at least one **condition** (`if … then`, ideally with `else`);
- at least one **event** besides the green flag (a key press, a sprite click, `ask … and wait`, or a broadcast).

Ideas: a cat that meows and counts every time you press space; a ball that bounces and scores when it touches a sprite; a three-question quiz that keeps score; a simplified [Oscartime](/scratch/oscartime-6.sb3) with your own sprites. Bigger is not better; **working** is better. The lecture's projects are on the [week 2 page](/weeks/week-02#how-this-page-is-organized) if you want to open one and change it, but the submitted project must be your own build.

When it runs: **Share → Copy Link**, paste it after `Project:` in `README.md`, and write one or two sentences under it saying what the program does and which custom block, variable, loop, condition and event it uses. The checks look for the link and at least twenty words.

</Step>

<Step n={7} title="Part C · Bits and bytes" time="15 min" id="part-c">

Open `answers.txt`. Seven questions from the first half of the lecture: three numbers to write in binary, one binary number to read, two ASCII bytes to decode, and two facts. Write each answer after the `=` sign, nothing else on the line.

Do them **on paper first** with the place values 128 64 32 16 8 4 2 1, then check yourself with the bit widget on the [week 2 page](/weeks/week-02#part-2-representation-everything-is-bits). The ASCII table is at [asciitable.com](https://www.asciitable.com/); case matters.

<Terminal cmd="python3 check.py" out={`:) Part C: 13, 42 and 100 in binary (q1–q3)
:) Part C: 10101010 in decimal (q4) and the ASCII text 01001000 01101001 (q5)
:) Part C: values in a byte (q6) and binary-search steps for a billion (q7)`} />

</Step>

<Step n={8} title="Part D · Pseudocode" time="10 min" id="part-d">

Open `pseudocode.txt`. Pick **one** of the three problems in the file and write your algorithm as numbered steps, the way we wrote the phone-book algorithm: at least six lines, at least one `if`, and at least one loop (`go back to line N`, `repeat`, `while` or `until`). Delete the example first; the checks refuse it.

Be precise enough for a robot that understands nothing. "Find the biggest" is not a step; "if this number is bigger than the one I remember, remember this one" is.

</Step>

<Step n={9} title="Submit" time="1 min" id="submit">

**Submitting = pushing your work to GitHub.** Type these three lines in the terminal:

<Terminal lines={['git add -A', 'git commit -m "Lab 1"', 'git push']} />

Prefer buttons? Use the **Source Control** icon on the left of VS Code: type a message, click **Commit**, then **Sync Changes**. (If you edited the files on github.com with the pencil, each **Commit changes** already is a push.)

Then check that it arrived:

1. Open your repository on github.com. Your latest commit message (“Lab 1”) is shown at the top of the file list.
2. Next to it, after about a minute, a **✅** (all checks passed) or **❌** (some failed) appears. Click it, then **Details**, to see the same `:)` / `:(` list as `python3 check.py`.

</Step>

## How to submit

> [!note] The short version
> 1. Your repository is `your-username/ics-2026-lab01`, **private**, with **drferhatu** added as a collaborator.
> 2. Your **name and student ID** are in `README.md`.
> 3. You **push** before **Friday, October 2, 23:59**. Push as often as you like; your **last push before the deadline** is graded.

Good to know:

- **What is graded.** After the deadline the instructor runs the **official tests** on your last push, so changing the files in `tests/` does not change your grade. `python3 check.py` shows you exactly what the grader will see. The two Scratch projects are also opened and looked at.
- **Late work.** Pushes up to five days late are graded with 10% off per day, as in the syllabus. After that, nothing is accepted.
- **Time stamps.** The deadline is checked against the time GitHub **received** your push, not the clock on your computer.
- **Working with others.** Discuss ideas, not solutions. The project and the answers must be yours, and you must be able to explain them.

## Troubleshooting

> [!note] I cannot find “Create your copy”, or GitHub shows a 404
> Sign in to GitHub first, then click the button again. You can also open [the template repository](https://github.com/FiratUniversity-IJDP-SoftEng/ics-2026-lab01-template) and click **Use this template → Create a new repository**.

> [!note] I named my repository wrongly or made it public
> Open **Settings → General**. Rename it to `ics-2026-lab01` at the top, and under **Danger Zone → Change visibility** make it **Private**. Your work is not lost.

> [!note] I cannot find `drferhatu` when adding a collaborator
> Type the username exactly, without `@`. If GitHub asks for your password or a code, that is a normal security check.

> [!note] The Share button is greyed out in Scratch
> New Scratch accounts need a confirmed email before sharing. Confirm it, or ask the instructor and hand in the project file instead (**File → Save to your computer**) for now.

> [!note] `git push` says “rejected” or “fetch first”
> You edited the repository on github.com (for example the README) after opening the codespace. Run `git pull`, then `git push` again.

> [!note] I see ❌ next to my commit
> Your push arrived; some checks still fail. Run `python3 check.py` to see which ones, fix them, and push again.
