## What happened on Monday, September 21

The first meeting was the introduction: who we are, what the course is, and how the semester runs. This page is the written version, for anyone who missed it and for everyone to come back to.

## The one question

Everything a computer does, from a text message to a self-driving car, comes down to this:

> [!definition] Computer science
> The study of **information**: how to represent it and how to process it. Computation is **input → algorithm → output**. Computational thinking is applying that lens to problems you care about.

A computer has no idea what a photo, a song or a word is. It has electricity that is either on or off, which we call **1** and **0**. The whole course is the story of how we build everything else out of that.

## How the course runs

| When | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Before Monday | Watch the week's **CS50 lecture** (link on the week page), read the notes, skim the slides | at home, edX or YouTube |
| Monday 13:30–14:45 | **Lecture**: the ideas, deeper and faster because you have watched CS50, with live coding and industry examples | B101 |
| Monday from 15:00 | **Lab**: you write code, mostly in C, with autograded feedback. You can start before the instructor arrives; the lab page tells you everything | YM LAB |
| After class | Finish and push the lab, then watch next week's CS50 lecture | anywhere |

The official slot is 13:15–17:00. We start the lecture at 13:30.

> [!note] Why CS50?
> Harvard's CS50 is the most watched introduction to computer science in the world, and its materials are free and excellent. You are enrolled in **CS50x 2026 on edX**. We follow its order, use its tools (VS Code in the browser, the CS50 library, the duck), and add our own labs, projects and industry context. Watching the lecture at home means we do not waste Monday on things a recording does better.

## What you need before week 2

1. A **GitHub account** (free). Choose a username you would put on a CV.
2. Accept the invitation to the course organization on GitHub (arrives by email).
3. Sign in once to **Classroom 50**.

All three steps are in the [lab setup guide](/guides/lab-setup). Ten minutes, no installation.

## Grading, briefly

- **Midterm** (week 8): 40%.
- **Final**: 60%, made of the final exam (85% of that) and the **semester project** (15% of that).
- **Labs**: autograded, tracked, and the practice ground for everything else. A student who does every lab has never failed this course.
- The [project catalog](/project) is published in week 2. You choose in week 4, you build from week 5, you demo in week 15.

## The road ahead

| Weeks | Module | You will |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Foundations | See how everything becomes bits; program in Scratch |
| 3–4 | The C language | Write real programs: variables, loops, functions, arrays, strings |
| 5–8 | Algorithms, memory, data structures | Search, sort, use pointers, build linked lists and hash tables; midterm in week 8 |
| 9–11 | Python, AI and data | Do in ten lines what took fifty in C; understand LLMs; query databases |
| 12–13 | The web | Build a page with HTML, CSS and JavaScript; see how the Internet moves it |
| 14–15 | Humans and projects | Design for people; demo your project |
